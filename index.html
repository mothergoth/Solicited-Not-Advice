If you're here, a debt collector has probably contacted you — and you're trying to figure out what to do.

Welcome. I've been through this too — including a collector who told me federal law required me to pay a debt that Texas law says they couldn't legally collect. This site is what I put together along the way: how to recognize bad-faith tactics, what the law actually says, and concrete next steps.

— built by someone who's been on the other end of that phone call

This is not legal advice. This is a free, community-built guide meant to help you understand your rights and figure out what to do next — not a substitute for a licensed attorney. Laws change, and every situation is different. If you're facing a lawsuit, wage garnishment, or a large or complicated debt, please talk to a licensed attorney or a free legal aid organization in your state. Using this site does not create an attorney-client relationship with anyone.

What this site is (and isn't)

Everything on this site is backed by an actual statute, regulation, or federal agency source — you can check every one of them yourself on the Sources page. Where I couldn't independently confirm something, I left it out rather than guess. That's a deliberate choice: a wrong citation in a letter to a collector can hurt your position more than no citation at all.

What this site can't do is know your specific situation. It's a map, not a lawyer. If things escalate — a lawsuit, a judgment, wage garnishment — please get a real one.

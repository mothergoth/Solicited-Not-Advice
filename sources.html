Sources

Every legal claim on this site links back to a primary source — a statute, regulation, or federal agency document — so you can check it yourself. Nothing on this site is published without a source; where something couldn't be independently confirmed, it was left out rather than published as a guess.

These codes and statutes may not reflect the most current version. Texas, California, New York, and other states referenced here may have more current or accurate information than what's summarized. We recommend you verify against official government sources before relying on this entirely for your own situation. Further, your state AG might provide sources and programs to assist you with debt collections situations — it is important you contact and review the available information in your jurisdiction.

What's deliberately not on this site

A few things were researched but excluded because they couldn't be independently confirmed against a primary source:

  • Specific Texas balance-billing dollar protections (the mediation process under Insurance Code Chapter 1467 is confirmed, but the actual billing limits live in separate sections not yet verified)
  • Whether individual state medical-debt credit-reporting bans survive a July 2025 federal court ruling — this remains legally unsettled
  • Debt-buyer chain-of-title pleading requirements under New York civil procedure law
  • Texas repossession sale-proceeds accounting requirements (Bus. & Com. Code §9.615)
  • Statute-of-limitations figures for any state besides Texas, California, and New York

If any of these matter to your situation, that's a good reason to talk to an attorney or your state's legal aid organization — not to guess based on what's missing here.
